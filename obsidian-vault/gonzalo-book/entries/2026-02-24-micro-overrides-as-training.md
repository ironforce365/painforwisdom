## Core Insight
The override muscle doesn't care about the size of the task -- every postponed email, avoided conversation, or deferred errand is a rep that builds the same mental capacity as a storm run, because the aMCC responds to the act of overriding avoidance, not to the difficulty of the challenge.

## Story Anchor
Gonzalo describes his own practice of sitting without his phone, noticing recurring thoughts -- particularly tasks he keeps postponing -- and forcing himself to do them regardless of whether he understands why he's avoiding them. He states directly: "that happened to me -- growth will come before you even realize, and when it seems hard, it starts to look less hard." The growth came not from dramatic physical challenges but from the accumulated effect of small overrides.

## Framework Connection
This entry deepens the [[amcc-effect]] framework by making its most accessible case: the aMCC override mechanism does not distinguish between running in freezing rain and sending a postponed email. Both are reps. This also extends [[friction-types]] by identifying a new category of growth friction hiding in plain sight -- administrative and social micro-tasks that people dismiss as "too small to matter" but that are in fact the most available daily training ground.

## Practical Application
Set a 2-minute phone-free check-in with yourself (Gonzalo suggests daily). Ask: "What am I postponing?" Pick one task -- it can be as small as sending an email or making a phone call. Do it without analyzing the resistance. Don't negotiate with the avoidance, just override it. Track these micro-overrides for a week and notice how the threshold for "hard" starts shifting downward. This is aMCC training without needing a track, a gym, or bad weather.

## Who It's For
The person who believes they need a dramatic challenge to grow -- who scrolls past Goggins content feeling like they can't access that level of difficulty, while ignoring the email they've been avoiding for three days. Also for the runner or athlete who trains hard physically but avoids small life friction (administrative tasks, uncomfortable conversations, decisions they keep deferring). They have the override capacity but only deploy it in domains that feel "worthy."

## Integrity Check

The claim that small overrides build the same capacity as large ones is supported by the aMCC research framework he references consistently.

### Angle 2: Micro-habits and behavioral accumulation
*Why this matters for this entry: If small overrides compound into capacity, the habit formation literature should explain the mechanism.*

**Category A -- Comprehensive understanding**

Paper: "How are habits formed: Modelling habit formation in the real world" by Lally, van Jaarsveld, Potts, Wardle
- European Journal of Social Psychology, vol. 40, pp. 998-1009, 2010
- Relevance: Landmark study showing habit formation takes 18-254 days and that missing a single repetition does not derail the process -- supports the idea that consistent small overrides compound into automatic capacity

- Chapter 6: "Can Willpower Be Strengthened?"
- Relevance: Covers evidence that small acts of self-control (correcting posture, tracking spending) build general willpower capacity across unrelated domains -- directly supports the micro-override thesis
- Verified: Yes

## Core Insight
Real mental toughness is not about refusing to slow down — it is about choosing to stay in the hard environment and adjusting your effort without quitting or pretending it is easy.

## Story Anchor
Gonzalo is running trails in a heat advisory, 90 degrees at noon. He is climbing a hill and walking it. Instead of framing walking as failure or pushing through recklessly, he says: "I'm walking, but that's fine, because as I said, it's hard." He chose to be out there on purpose. He chose to walk the climb without ego. Both decisions — showing up and adjusting — are the training.

## Framework Connection
This connects directly to the aMCC effect: the override muscle does not care about split times — it cares that you overrode the impulse to skip entirely. It also maps to the three modes framework — walking the hill with full awareness and acceptance is conscious presence, not cruise-control quitting or rigid ego-driven pushing. The distinction between strategic and manufactured suffering is sharp here: adjusting pace in extreme heat is strategic (staying in the fight), while pushing through recklessly would be manufactured suffering (protecting ego at the cost of the actual goal).

## Practical Application
Next time conditions are terrible (weather, fatigue, life stress), go do the session anyway but give yourself explicit permission to adjust intensity. The goal is not the pace or the performance — the goal is showing up when your brain is screaming to stay comfortable. Walk the hill if you need to. The aMCC does not care about your split times; it cares that you overrode the impulse to skip entirely. Train the override muscle, not the ego.

## Who It's For
The runner (or anyone pursuing something hard) who skips sessions when conditions are not ideal — waiting for perfect weather, perfect energy, perfect schedule. They confuse "I can only train when things are right" with discipline, when real discipline is showing up imperfectly and staying in the fight with adjusted expectations.

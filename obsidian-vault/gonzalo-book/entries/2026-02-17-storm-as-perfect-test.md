## Core Insight
The conditions that make you want to skip the workout are often the exact conditions you need to train in -- and recognizing them as "perfect" instead of hostile is what separates strategic preparation from comfort-seeking.

## Story Anchor
Gonzalo saw a winter storm alert, rain all day, muddy trails -- and his reaction was "it was perfect. It was perfect for a test." He then deliberately stacked the difficulty by choosing the steepest trail in his area on top of the bad weather. He did not stumble into this discomfort; he selected it with intent.

## Framework Connection
This entry directly illustrates the [[strategic-vs-manufactured-suffering]] distinction. Gonzalo chose the storm not to prove toughness but because storms happen on race day -- this is strategic suffering. He also stacked difficulty (steepest trail + bad weather), which connects to the [[amcc-effect]]: voluntary discomfort chosen deliberately builds override capacity.

## Practical Application
Next time conditions turn against your training plan -- bad weather, fatigue, a rough day at work -- pause before skipping. Ask: "Is this actually a perfect test?" If you are training for something real (a race, a hard conversation, a career move), the hostile conditions might be exactly the simulation you need. Stack one additional challenge on top (harder route, longer distance, no music) to build your override capacity deliberately. This is not about punishing yourself -- it is about recognizing that the discomfort you are avoiding in training is the same discomfort you will face on race day, and you want to have already met it.

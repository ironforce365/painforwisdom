## Core Insight
Growth hides inside the conditions most people optimize away -- the deliberate choice to seek harder conditions when easier ones are available is what separates strategic discomfort from passive training.

## Story Anchor
"It's winter but in sunny San Diego it's almost 90 degrees, so what I do then -- I pick the warmest time of the day and I go for a long run." Gonzalo does not stumble into heat; he scans his environment, identifies where the discomfort lives, and moves toward it on purpose. He inverts the normal optimization: instead of picking the coolest window to make the run easier, he picks the hottest window to make it count.

## Framework Connection
This connects directly to the aMCC effect: voluntary hard choices, repeated consistently, build the override muscle that transfers across every domain. It also maps to the distinction between growth friction (productive, chosen) and comfort-seeking defaults. The entry draws a clear line between strategic suffering (picking the heat with purpose) and manufactured suffering (making things harder for ego).

## Practical Application
Before your next training session (or any recurring challenge), ask: "Am I choosing the version that builds capacity, or the version that protects comfort?" This is not about making everything harder for its own sake -- it is about identifying the one variable you can shift toward growth friction and deliberately choosing it. Stack conditions with intent: if the run is long, make it hot. If the meeting is tough, go in first. If the day is already hard, do the hardest task now. The principle is the same -- stop optimizing for ease and start optimizing for growth.

## Who It's For
The person who trains consistently but always under comfortable conditions -- they run early to avoid heat, they pick the flat route, they schedule hard conversations for "later." They are doing the work but unconsciously filtering out the discomfort that would actually build capacity. They are leaving growth on the table without realizing it.
